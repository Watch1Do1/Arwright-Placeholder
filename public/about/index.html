About Arwright

Arwright is a writing-process platform for high schools that helps students use AI responsibly without losing their own thinking. Instead of writing or rewriting student work, it coaches students and shows teachers how a piece of writing was actually built: drafts, revisions, pastes, and AI coach conversations.

Who is Arwright for?

  • High school teachers in any subject that assigns writing
  • School and district leaders setting AI and academic-integrity policy
  • Students who want to grow as writers and keep a portfolio of their work

What does it do?

  • In-house word processor that captures the writing process
  • AI writing coach that guides students with questions and feedback, not ghostwriting
  • Process replay so teachers can watch how a draft developed
  • Writing Process Review that gives teachers evidence, not verdicts. The teacher makes the call.
  • Students reflect on their own thinking and can see their own growth
  • Classes and assignments for teachers

What is Arwright's approach?

Arwright focuses on teaching responsible AI use rather than catching students. AI detectors tend to create mistrust; Arwright gives teachers a picture of real student growth. Our guiding principle: measure the work, not the student.

Does Arwright write essays for students?

No. The AI coach asks questions, points to places in the draft, and suggests next steps. Students do the writing, and the coach never writes text into the essay.

Is Arwright an AI detector?

No. Arwright shows how a piece of writing developed. That process is evidence for the teacher, and the teacher makes the call. The Writing Process Review never determines misconduct.

What can teachers see about the writing process?

For students in their own classes, teachers can see a replay of how the paper developed, its revisions, any pasted text, the questions the student asked the AI coach, and the student's own reflection.

Can students see how their writing develops over time?

Yes. Your Writing Journey gives each student a page with one chapter per paper: how they built it, what they asked the coach about, their first and final versions, and their reflection. It shares observations only, with no scores, rankings, or comparisons with classmates.

What is Arwright's status?

Live and ready for the pilot stage, starting with high schools. A study of how students use AI in writing is being prepared for IRB submission.
